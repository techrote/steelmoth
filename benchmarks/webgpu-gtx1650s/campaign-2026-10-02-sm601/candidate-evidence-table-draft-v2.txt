The deferred study demonstrates a 1.129876 ms mean queue-span reduction in its
isolated synthetic workload. The counter study demonstrates a 0.021961 ms mean
temporal-kernel reduction (2.988%) in its separate workload. Their gains must not
be added. Native four-way comparison is still required before adoption.

The studies do not measure complete command spans. Their commandGpuMs is null;
their pass times are neither summed nor subtracted to manufacture command time.
Main measures both queue span and complete coverage of two explicit command
submissions, including diagnostic copy and observer overhead. The 1.100543 ms
command result leaves the real 3.067721 ms queue-latency result intact.

Main separately measures CPU callback wall time of 3.802611 ms, the known stats
map wait of 3.592167 ms, and timestamp-map transport of 0.607111 ms. These overlap
GPU execution and are never subtracted from a GPU timestamp. All direct GPU
intervals were independently recomputed from retained nanosecond pairs. All
command sums, alias fields and exported per-run summaries match their samples.

The #105 overlay caches five input states (414,720,000 host typed-array bytes at
1080p) while preserving original upload bytes, shader/timing core and per-frame
operations. This is a separately identified source, with changed inter-iteration
CPU scheduling. All three runs pass 56 exact four-texture/eight-counter-word
cases and bounded-slot saturation. The #104 overlay changes only Windows startup
readiness; all three runs pass 56 exact cases with two repeats per case. Main
retains 1,800 valid frames, three distinct process IDs, empty error lists and
observed history rejection. Human visual acceptance remains explicitly false.
